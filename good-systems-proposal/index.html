You said this one matters
Q

Does Milo respect Snowflake's role-based access controls, or does it query as a single service account?

M

Both are possible, but role-based access control is the default. Milo is built so people only see the data they are permitted to see, row-level security included.

That means last week's company-wide RBAC rollout carries straight through rather than being flattened into one service account. If you ever decide you want a single service account instead, that is a configuration choice rather than a rebuild.

Q

Is there a native Brandwatch connector, or is it CSV import?

M

A connector, never a CSV import. You will have a working Brandwatch connector in place by the end of the pilot, so social listening, sentiment and influencer discovery sit in the same workspace as paid spend, across global and local-language markets.

We do not rely on CSV import for anything. Where a connector is not already in place we write one, usually in one to two days, and it is included in the proof of concept rather than quoted separately. The same applies to Microsoft Store, Statsig and Profound, the other three on your list.

Q

Where would unstructured data live? We run events globally, and have no system of record for them at all.

M

Milo ingests unstructured data directly, pushed in through Google Drive, email and other routes, so the events programme gets a system of record it does not currently have.

This is the one item on your list where Milo is not replacing a tool but filling a gap, which also makes it the easiest thing to judge during the pilot. There is no incumbent to compare it against, so cafe takeovers, campus tours and press events either become answerable alongside paid spend or they do not.

Q

How does Milo sit alongside Tableau and Hex rather than duplicating them? Our Insights team will ask.

M

Honestly, there is overlap, and it would be strange to pretend otherwise. The difference is that Milo brings its own data platform: it combines sources that are currently isolated, then makes the result answerable in plain English by anyone.

Nothing has to be switched off. Run all three in parallel through the pilot and let the Insights team judge it on use rather than on a pitch. Our experience is that teams stop reaching for Tableau and Hex over time, but that should be their conclusion after four weeks, not a migration you commit to up front.
