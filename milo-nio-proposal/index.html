01 · From order email to invoice

Dashboards are the easy part. This is the doing.

A trade order comes in by email. Milo checks it against recipes and stock, confirms the date with the customer, sends it to fulfilment and raises the invoice in Xero at that customer's prices. People step in where you've said they should, and nowhere else.

ORDEREmail lands. Lines and date read.
CHECKRecipes against Horsham stock.
CONFIRMCustomer told the pick date.
SHIPOrder sent to fulfilment.
INVOICERaised in Xero at their prices.
How it's written in Milo

When a trade order arrives by email:

  1. Read the lines and the delivery date.
  2. Check every cocktail against recipes and Horsham stock.
    • All there: confirm, send to fulfilment, invoice.
    • A liquid short: plan the split, draft the PO, ask ops.
    • No price in Xero: stop and ask finance.
  3. Post every step in the ops channel.

Each fork is a rule you set in plain English. Change a rule and the flow changes. Zoho is only ever read, never written to.

02 · What Milo works from

What lives in the spreadsheets and in people's heads.

The answers above only work because Milo knows how NIO buys, makes and ships. That knowledge sits in one place, written down once and kept current, rather than rebuilt by hand every time a new order changes the plan.

WhatHeld asFor example

Recipes

Every cocktail as a 100 ml build, one row per SKU. Change a SKU once and every forecast uses the change.

Suppliers and lead times

Who supplies each liquid, how long it takes and what drives the wait.

Cocchi vermouth: harvest, pressing, then a three week truck. Six weeks.

Buying rules

When to round up, when to top up, and what never spoils.

Spirits: buy the IBC when six months of forecast covers it. Perishables: buy to the run.

Freight

Direct or consolidated, cost against days.

Consolidated by default. Direct only when the date needs it.
