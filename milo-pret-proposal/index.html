Three questions for the ops meeting

Answered in the room, with the why.

Each one draws on more than one of Pret's systems, which is why nobody can answer it from a dashboard today. The figures are illustrative.

1
Which shops opened in the last year are behind their sales ramp, and what is different about them?
Draws onSimphonyNetSuite
What Milo answersNine of the 31 shops opened since last September are behind the ramp for their age, and seven of the nine are roadside. Breakfast and lunch are on the curve. The gap is the afternoon: roadside sites take 22% of the day between two and five o'clock against 31% at high-street shops of the same age, because the range was built for a lunch queue and the afternoon customer is buying a coffee and something small.
The decision it unblocksRebase the roadside targets on a roadside curve, and fix the afternoon range before anyone judges the sites against the wrong benchmark.
2
Why did average serve time rise in the Manchester cluster last week?
Draws onSimphonyHarri
What Milo answersServe time rose 41 seconds across the cluster and two shops explain three quarters of it. Piccadilly and Deansgate were each one barista short at the morning peak on Tuesday, Wednesday and Friday against the Harri template, and hot-drink lines per basket rose 18% after the new drinks launch, which lengthens every order. The other nine shops were flat.
The decision it unblocksA rota conversation with two shop managers, not a region-wide fix.
3
Which shops have the highest barista turnover, and is it showing up in serve times?
Draws onWorkdayHarriSimphony
The question only a joined-up warehouse can answer.
What Milo answersFourteen shops have barista turnover above 60% on a rolling twelve months. In eleven of them the morning peak runs 30 to 50 seconds slower than shops of the same format. Workday shows the leavers are in their first six months, Harri shows those shops leaning on new starters at eight o'clock, and Simphony shows the slowdown is on hot drinks, not food.
The decision it unblocksPoint barista training and retention effort at the eleven shops where it moves serve time, rather than at the whole estate.

These are our questions. Yours are better, and that is what the first conversation is for.

Where it fits at Pret

Ops first. Power BI goes.

Milo does not replace Simphony, Harri, NetSuite or Snowflake. It replaces the layer on top of them: the dashboards, the weekly pack, and the questions that used to become tickets.

Ops leadership and area managers first.

The people in the Tuesday meeting and the people driving between shops. Start there, and the questions they ask in month one shape what comes next.

Power BI goes.

Milo has its own dashboards and builds apps for the ops team. The Tuesday pack, a new-openings tracker, a serve-time leaderboard: built by asking, kept current on their own, and opened by the people who asked for them. One layer instead of two.

Copilot stays.

Copilot knows your documents. Milo knows your numbers. They get asked different things, and neither has to pretend to be the other.

One question a week, not one ticket.

The requests that used to become dashboard tickets get answered where they are asked. The data team keeps the warehouse and loses the report queue.

For the data team

Milo sits on the warehouse they built, uses the definitions they set, and gets their work in front of more people, faster. They keep Snowflake and the pipelines. They stop maintaining a dashboard layer on top of them.
