Security · The closed loop

Your data stays yours. Full stop.

This is where most AI tools fall down, and why regulated gaming and lottery operators across Europe run on Milo. The whole system is a closed loop: no third parties, no training on your data, no copy of your warehouse.

No storage, no training

  • Read-only connection. Milo never trains on your data.
  • Nothing is stored. Dashboards use short-lived caching for speed, nothing permanent.
  • The one exception is chat history, kept so users have their threads. Delete a thread and it is gone.

No third parties

  • Nothing goes to OpenAI or any external AI service.
  • All models run inside Milo's own AWS environment under a zero data retention agreement.
  • Even logging and monitoring are in-house. Your data never touches a third-party system.

Private by default

  • Threads are never shared between users.
  • Memory is per-user and visible to that user.
  • Shared knowledge is opt-in only: Milo asks before anything goes into the company knowledge base, and access to it is role-controlled.

Access control, down to the row

  • Role-based access control: build any role, attach any mix of permissions.
  • Row-level security per brand and team, so people only see what they are entitled to.
  • Single sign-on as standard. Microsoft Entra enterprise controls on request.

Usage · No surprises

What counts, what doesn't.

One number drives the cost: interactions. An interaction is Milo doing fresh data analysis with AI. Everything else is free. No seat licences, no hidden extras.

Counts
  • Asking Milo a question that pulls or analyses data.
  • Building something new with AI, such as designing a report or dashboard with prompts.
  • An automation that specifically calls on AI when it runs.
Doesn't count
  • Follow-up questions on data Milo has already pulled.
  • Opening, viewing, or refreshing dashboards, however many times.
  • Scheduled sends of a report already built, like the daily exec brief.
  • Users, dashboards, and queries: unlimited.
