Dear Ben,

Peppermint's March piece on data and consumer insight put it well: “By the time you're looking at post-event data, you're a year too late.” EPOS shows what sold, when the peaks came and which bars underperformed. It can't show why.

You already have the hard part. Peppermint has more than 20 years of EPOS data, over 100 million transactions and 2,000 terminals of its own. The reasons sit next to that data, in stock, rotas, ticketing, payments, the site network and the weather, each in its own system.

Milo connects them. Anyone at Peppermint, from a bar manager on site to the finance director, asks a question in plain English and gets the answer in seconds, with its sources. A question can keep running as an alert during a show, and the client report can draft itself afterwards.

Given your remit across payments, ticketing, connectivity and new tech, the demo below starts at a technology seat. Everything runs on sample data, so click around.

Getting started

Start in the off-season. Plan 2027 on it.

The festival season is over for the year, and the venues and winter markets are just starting. Connect this season's data now, and next year's plan is built on it.

STEP 1

Connect

Start with one event's EPOS, stock and rotas, on read-only access. Last season's data works fine.

STEP 2

Define

We teach Milo Peppermint's terms: spend per head, pars, bar numbers, and how each commercial model settles.

STEP 3

Set access

A bar manager sees their bar, an account manager their clients, a promoter their own events, and finance everything.

STEP 4

Ask, then act

Answers start the day the connections are live, in Teams, Slack, WhatsApp or the browser.
