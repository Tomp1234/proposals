Dear Lee,

Congratulations on Refrigeration Product of the Year at the National ACR & Heat Pump Awards in June. Quickfridge sells time: a failed room back to temperature in 20 minutes, so the repair can happen when it suits.

The business behind it runs on time too. A unit on the shelf in Glasgow doesn't help a pub cellar in Southampton during a heatwave. Knowing where the fleet should be means joining hire records, the centres' stock, website enquiries, support calls and the ledger, usually by hand.

Milo connects them. Anyone at Quickfridge asks a question in plain English and gets the answer in seconds, with its sources. A question can keep running, so the Monday fleet plan and the monthly figures build themselves.

Those same records are also the best brief for the next version of the unit, so the demo below starts at the innovation seat. Everything runs on sample data, so click around.

Getting started

Ready before the Christmas bank holidays.

Your site says a breakdown never picks a good time: the summer rush, big events, bank holidays. Connect this summer's hires now and the Christmas fleet plan is a question, not a ring-round. By May, Milo has a full year to plan the summer from. Four steps, borrowed from your own set-up guide.

STEP 1

Plug it in

Connect the enquiry forms, hire records, the unit register and the ledger, on read-only access. This summer's data works fine.

STEP 2

Set the temperature

We teach Milo Quickfridge's terms: hire weeks, room volume in m³, cellar, chiller and freezer modes, centres, deposits and install kits.

STEP 3

Leave the right space

A centre sees its own units, a contractor its own hires, the service lead every unit, and the directors everything.

STEP 4

Leave it alone

Answers start the day the connections are live. The questions that matter keep running, in Teams, WhatsApp, email or the browser.
