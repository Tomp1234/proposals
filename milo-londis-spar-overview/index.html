The shift

From exports and pivots to a question and an answer.

How it usually works
  • Each EPOS reports on its own store and nothing beyond it
  • Londis and SPAR classify products differently, so like for like is a manual job
  • Symbol group reporting arrives lagged, in a fixed shape you cannot question
  • Promotion funding and rebates get reconciled in a spreadsheet, if at all
  • Concession income sits apart from retail sales and rarely meets it
  • By the time a report is built, the trading week has already moved on
With Milo
  • One layer across every fascia, every store, every week
  • Category hierarchies mapped once, so the two estates compare properly
  • Ask in plain English, get an answer in seconds with sources attached
  • Supplier funding checked against what actually sold, automatically
  • Retail, concessions, delivery and costs in the same view
  • Answers are live, and the recurring ones send themselves
The walkthrough

Five questions from five different people.

Each of these is a question somebody in a convenience estate asks and then waits days for. These are illustrations, not results. Week one of a pilot is where they get asked of real store data.

Store owner
"Which lines lost me margin this month, and was it price or promotion?"
Splits margin movement into cost price changes and discount given away, line by line. Shows where a promotion sold volume that was never going to pay for itself.
Food to go
"What is waste running at on fresh and food to go by day, and which day am I over-producing?"
Waste against sales by daypart and day of week, per site. Usually names one or two specific days carrying most of the loss rather than a general problem.
Availability
"Which of my top 200 lines went out of stock last week, and what did it cost in sales?"
Gap on shelf inferred from till data and ordering, priced at normal rate of sale. Turns availability from a feeling into a number with a value on it.
Finance
"Weekly gross margin by store, with concession income shown separately."
Retail margin and concession income side by side rather than blended. Makes it obvious which stores are actually trading well and which are being carried by footfall services.
Across the portfolio
"Rank every store I represent on like for like growth, split by fascia."
One league table across both symbol groups, normalised so the comparison holds. The view nobody can produce today without a week of manual work.

Each client sees their own stores. You see all of them.

Which means Milo does not have to be something you run on your clients' behalf. It can be something you put in front of them, and a revenue line for you rather than a cost.

How a pilot runs

One month, one or two stores, real data.

We would start with a small, representative slice rather than the whole portfolio. One Londis site and one SPAR site is usually enough to prove the point, because the hard part is making two different structures agree.
